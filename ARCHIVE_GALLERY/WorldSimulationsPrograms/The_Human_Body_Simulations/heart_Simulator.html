Anatomy note
In an anterior view, the right ventricle forms most of the front surface of the heart. The left ventricle forms the left border and apex. The left atrium lies mostly posteriorly, so only part of it is prominent from the front.
Blood pathway
Body → right atriumSuperior and inferior vena cava return deoxygenated blood.
Right ventricle → lungsBlood exits through the pulmonary valve into the pulmonary trunk and arteries.
Lungs → left atriumPulmonary veins return oxygenated blood.
Left ventricle → bodyBlood exits through the aortic valve into the aorta.

How to read this illustration

Blue represents oxygen-poor blood; red represents oxygen-rich blood. In a real heart, all chambers contain blood throughout the cycle, and circulation is continuous. The moving particles emphasize the predominant direction of flow—not actual individual blood cells or measured velocities.

What changes during a beat

Ventricular contraction increases pressure. The mitral and tricuspid valves close before the pulmonary and aortic valves open. Relaxation reverses the sequence.

Ventricular fillingTricuspid and mitral valves are open while the aortic and pulmonary valves remain closed.
DiastasisMid-diastolic filling slows as atrial and ventricular pressures approach each other.
Atrial systoleAtria contract late in diastole and complete ventricular filling.
Isovolumetric contractionAll four valves are briefly closed while ventricular pressure rises rapidly.
EjectionAortic and pulmonary valves open once ventricular pressure exceeds pressure in the aorta and pulmonary artery.
